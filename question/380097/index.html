Asked by Dynaflock on 2021-06-15 03:16:55 UTC

It sound like you have some kind of collision arbitration occurring on your communications similar to CAN bus where lower node IDs have priority. That said, you really should rethink your flocking algorithm. Swarming agents flock on prediction more than simple detection. There are always sensory and processing delays. The critical information in a received odometry message should not be when it is received, but rather, the time stamp showing when it was sent. If your problem is real world, you should expect to lose messages. If you really want to time synchronize then you allocate them a specific millisecond timeslot from a common start time offset by node id. I suspect your odometry transmission frequency is too high. Consider dropping your transmission frequency and include odometry and intention in a given agents periodic transmission. Also consider that natural flocking behaviour like murmurations are caused by local reactions to nearby agents, not the swarm.

Asked by Dynaflock on 2021-06-18 10:21:16 UTC

That would be domain specific. Whatever high frequency information exchange you have would work better with turtles than starlings. Also, since a flocking agents response is typically predicated on the relative direction angle to other agents then the separation of the agents will inversely effect angular delta and hence your response degradation due to a non-predictive strategy. The nearer they are the greater the inaccuracy obtainable from a non-predictive strategy.
