Inaccurate ForceTorque Feedback When Using Joint::SetVelocity Interface

We are currently setting the provide_feedback flag for a revolute joint in the sdf file of a robot model. The joint is being controlled by calling the Joint::SetVelocity function in order to emulate a position controlled servo using the ode physics. We would like to call Joint::GetForceTorque(0) to retrieve the forces and torques applied by the joint on its parent link. However, the data returned is obviously incorrect and randomly oscillates from -120 to 170 N when the robot is in static equilibrium. When controlling the servo using Joint::SetTorque, however, the data returned is accurate and reliable. Is there a known solution to accurately estimating forces and torques when setting joint velocities, as opposed to joint torques, using the ode physics?

It's possible the force and torque oscillations comes from parts of the model not behaving correctly. It's hard to diagnose the problem without knowing more details about the model.

In general, origin of errors could be numerical (temporal integrator or constraint solver based), or controller induced. Are you able to provide more details of the simulation you are running?

For reference, please take a look at the force torque unit test, there we measure simulated force torques under a few different setups/conditions and compare simulation results against analytical solutions. Not sure how similar/different the test worlds are compared to your model.

On a side note, we are looking at ways to expose diagnostics data that will help users debug their simulations. If you can compile gazebo from source, I can recommend some branches with experimental diagnostics tools that may help with this process.

Thanks for your response, and sorry for the delayed reply. We actually did some further testing based on what you said, and it turns out that the primary cause for the ForceTorque fluctuations was because of the oscillations of the robot. When better gains were selected that decreased shaking, the ForceTorque measurements were considerably more accurate. It also helped decreasing the physics time step a little bit, although this may have just further refined the correction.
