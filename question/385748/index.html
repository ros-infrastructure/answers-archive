For the assembler specifically, you'd have to update the position of the TF frame which is the origin of the LaserScans, otherwise the assembler will end up overlaying all the scans.

What about rotating the TF of your scanner by the amount the rotary has rotated? In a way, that's what's happening anyway: the location of the laser scanner, wrt the turntable, has changed (if you place a coordinate frame at the centre of the turntable and rotate it around, as the turntable is turning).

That would give the assembler a moving TF frame, and from there things should just work I believe (provided you have timestamped everything correctly and update the TF frames at a suitable rate).

re: calibration: that I can't help you with I'm afraid. In my comment I assume you have that information.

re: rotate the scans: no, you don't need to do that. You only need to make sure to publish the transform. The assembler does the transformation of the scans for you. That's sort of the point, otherwise the assembler would be almost nothing more than some really complex vector or list.
