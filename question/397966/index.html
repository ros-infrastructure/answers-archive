This produces a fairly decent result, but with rather large discreet jumps when the robot recieves another GPS message. I expect some jumps when it gets a fix, but I'd like to reduce the accumulating error as much as possible. I have a plot of a recent run where the robot is set up to do a 4km box and starts with a GPS position, surfaces on the second box leg (unintentioanlly, but it does get a new GPS fix), and finishes the mission at the end of the box close to the starting position, with a final GPS fix. The 2nd GPS fix in the middle of the run was unintentional, but did provide a source of error correction mid run and shows that the filter was still quite accurate. At the final position however, the discrete jump from the fix is quite large (close to 700 meters.) That's the floating dark green short segment near the start, which should connect to the large loop at the top of the graph.

Graph of Absolute(GPS) vs Local outputs of UKF nodes:

After looking through the data, I believe this error is caused by a Yaw/Heading issue. Based on the input heading data from the compass, the filter output orientation/heading seems to closely match. However, when calculating angle between two of the vectors (specifically the intersection of the lower left corner) from the plot, I notice a rough offset from the compass and localization heading of 15 degrees. This offset seems to show the angle as ~15 degrees more shallow than what the UKF output topic orientations show, or the compass heading provided. In this example, the filter has a delta heading of ~92deg from leg 2 to leg 3, while the delta heading from graph when calculated is ~77 on the same turn.

With my current filter set up, I don't see any spot where the offset/error could be coming from. Is this a problem with how heading is being included in the filter? I believe finding the source of the 15 degrees will line up the end of the mission much more closely. and reduce the overall error accumulation.
