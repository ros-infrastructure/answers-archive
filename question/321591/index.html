How do you compensate for IMU drift in pose estimation?

Hello,

I am attempting to create a full point cloud of a space utilizing a Velodyne VLP-16 LIDAR on a rotating lever arm to produce a more complete scan. I've attached a low cost IMU near the pivot point of the system, whereas the LIDAR is at the end of the lever arm.

The issue I am having is that the resultant scan, visualized in Rviz by setting the point cloud duration to a long time and viewing the resulting points, is warping as the arm is pitching. Through visual observation of the transform axis points in rviz, I realized there is some error in what the IMU is reporting for position. There is some drift when the system stops moving, and the rotation which ought to be only around one axis has slight rotation in another (producing the warping).

In ROS, I have setup the best transforms that I can to represent the distance away from the pivot, IMU, and Velodyne. I don't know how to get better measurements (or, solve for them with some calibration steps) which I imagine would help the issue, nor do I understand really a correct methodology for troubleshooting the setup of my system.

I think that I can solve the issue if I am able to process the resultant point clouds and correcting the IMU estimated pose every iteration. I have looked into utilizing ICP for this purpose, but I am not sure if this is the correct approach. I am also unsure if I need to be correcting the pose estimate during the scanning operation, or if it is sufficient to correct in the post-processing phase.

Thank you in advance for any advice you can give. I am trying my best to learn!
