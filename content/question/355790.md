  body_markdown: "I replicated your setup and used the gmapping node for SLAM with\
    \ your ground bag.\n\nNot quite sure what your problem is, but one issue I can\
    \ see is that to see the results of the SLAM algorithm running on your bag file\
    \ in RVIZ, you need to add the map message in RVIZ (Add -> rviz/Map), and then\
    \ select the /map topic. \nIf you do this, you will see that gmapping (for example)\
    \ is indeed generating a map from your bag file.\n\nAnother issue is the message\
    \ timestamps on your bag are set to the beginning of UNIX time (Jan 1 1970).\n\
    You can see the bag timestamps by running\n\n    rosbag info ground.bag\n\nand\
    \ looking at the \"start\" and \"end\" fields.\nThis is why you cannot see the\
    \ robot model in RVIZ even after including the \"RobotModel\" message, as ROS\
    \ drops old TF messages.\nYou can also see this in effect if you look at your\
    \ TF frames with\n\n    rosrun tf view_frames\n\nApart from map->odom (provided\
    \ by gmapping), all other transforms are 1593366155.891 seconds old.\n\nThis happens\
    \ because the Raspberry Pi 3 updates its date and time according to a time server\
    \ on the internet.\nIf the Pi is not connected to the internet, then it cannot\
    \ update its own time and thus defaults to the beginning of UNIX time.\nIn that\
    \ case, you need to set the date and time manually with the \"date\" command.\
    \ \nNote that the \"date\" command does not persist over reboots (the Pi 3 does\
    \ not have a battery backup for clock), so you have to do it every reboot.\n\n\
    I am not sure this is really a problem for you since the SLAM map is being generated\
    \ regardless of the old transforms.\nNevertheless, the easiest way to fix this\
    \ would be to update the date and time on your raspberry and record the bag again.\n\
    Another slightly less easy way to fix this is to use the ROSBAG API and update\
    \ the timestamps manually."
