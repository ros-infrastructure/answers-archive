  body_markdown: "Hi @masynthetic\n\nTo better answer your question, let's step back\
    \ a little to review the following concepts. Source: [ROS2 Documentation](https://docs.ros.org/en/galactic/Concepts/About-Executors.html?highlight=timer)\n\
    \n**Executors**:\n\n> An Executor uses one or more threads\n> of the underlying\
    \ operating system to\n> invoke the callbacks of subscriptions,\n> timers, service\
    \ servers, action\n> servers, etc. on incoming messages and\n> events. The explicit\
    \ Executor class\n> (in executor.hpp in rclcpp, in\n> executors.py in rclpy, or\
    \ in\n> executor.h in rclc) provides more\n> control over execution management\
    \ than\n> the spin mechanism in ROS 1, although\n> the basic API is very similar.\n\
    \n**Scheduling**\n\n> If the processing time of the\n> callbacks is shorter than\
    \ the period\n> with which messages and events occur,\n> the Executor basically\
    \ processes them\n> in FIFO order. However, if the\n> processing time of some\
    \ callbacks is\n> longer, messages and events will be\n> queued on the lower layers\
    \ of the\n> stack. The wait set mechanism reports\n> only very little information\
    \ about\n> these queues to the Executor. In\n> detail, it only reports whether\
    \ there\n> are any messages for a certain topic\n> or not. The Executor uses this\n\
    > information to process the messages\n> (including services and actions) in a\n\
    > round-robin fashion - but not in FIFO\n> order. In addition, **it prioritizes\
    \ all\n> timer events over the messages**. The\n> following flow diagram visualizes\
    \ this\n> scheduling semantics.\n\n![image description](https://ftp.osuosl.org/pub/ros/download.ros.org/downloads/se_migration/ros/16398592883005539.png)\n\
    \nThere is a great [presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sz-nllmtcc8&t=644s)\
    \ by Ralph Lange that explains Executor Design as well. And one key principle,\
    \ **Timers overrule everything** as there may be conflicting priorities in real\
    \ time with the other executors.\n\nGiven these 2 concepts of Executors and Scheduling,\
    \ we also need to appreciate that \n\n> \"ROS multiplexes independent message\n\
    > handlers onto shared threads using\n> custom scheduling policies.\n> Consequently,\
    \ applications running on\n> top of ROS are subject to the\n> scheduling decisions\
    \ of the underlying\n> operating system and the middleware\n> layer, with complex\
    \ and interdependent\n> effects on timing.\"\n\n**Source**: [Response-Time Analysis\
    \ of ROS 2 Processing\nChains Under Reservation-Based Schedulingby D. Casini,\
    \ T. Bla\xDF, I. L\xFCtkebohle, and B. B. Brandenburg](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/opus/volltexte/2019/10743/pdf/LIPIcs-ECRTS-2019-6.pdf)\n\
    \nTimers can be set in nano-seconds due to the important role they play in Scheduling,\
    \ thus the need to multiply by a million. Even though your application may not\
    \ need but the capability is there. \n\n\n"
