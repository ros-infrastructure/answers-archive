  body_markdown: "Hi @zhixin, let's start with `ros_control` package:\n\n> The ros_control\
    \ packages are a rewrite\n> of the pr2_mechanism packages to make\n> controllers\
    \ generic to all robots\n> beyond just the PR2.\n> \n> The ros_control packages\
    \ takes as\n> input the joint state data from your\n> robot's actuator's encoders\
    \ and an\n> input set point. It uses a generic\n> control loop feedback mechanism,\n\
    > typically a PID controller, to control\n> the output, typically effort, sent\
    \ to\n> your actuators. ros_control gets more\n> complicated for physical mechanisms\n\
    > that do not have one-to-one mappings\n> of joint positions, efforts, etc but\n\
    > these scenarios are accounted for\n> using transmission\n\nSource: http://wiki.ros.org/ros_control\n\
    One of the goals of ROS is not to re-invent the wheel so `ros_control` package\
    \ allows for wide range of applications.\nHowever it's not the only way to interact\
    \ with Hardware, you could do that through ROS1 messages and services.\nRefer\
    \ to this tutorial: http://wiki.ros.org/ROS/Tutorials/CreatingMsgAndSrv\n\nAnd\
    \ take a look at example of robot implementation, you can communicate with drivers\
    \ or other interfaces.\nhttps://docs.niryo.com/dev/ros/v3.2.0/en/source/stack_hardware/niryo_robot_hardware_interface.html#hardware-interface-node\n\
    \nFinally I suggest you look at what is also available with ROS2 control: http://control.ros.org/\n\
    \n"
