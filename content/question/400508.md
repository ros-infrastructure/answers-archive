    While everything is possible, I would advise you to find the original kuka controller for your robot. If you were to build a complete new controller from scratch there's quite a bit of stuff you'll have to reverse engineer. Also, most robotic arms have some sort of embedded and/or real-time motion execution controller. ROS is usually used to send high level commands to the internal execution controller of the robot.

  body_markdown: "I'd tend to agree with @Alex-SSoM: if you don't have experience\
    \ with this sort of thing, or the drive to gain that experience, it's going to\
    \ be a difficult process. Unfortunately there isn't really a \"ROS package for\
    \ retrofitting old industrial robots/controllers\".\n\nHowever, if you like reverse\
    \ engineering things, and have access or can acquire the necessary parts, it *can*\
    \ be done.\n\nTo make this a little more than a one-line answer, two recent examples:\n\
    \n### Tormach ZA6\n\nLink: [Tormach ZA6](https://tormach.com/machines/robots.html).\n\
    \nAn (older) presentation about it from the ROS-Industrial youtube channel: [Tormach\
    \ - A ROS-Based Open Industrial Manipulator](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFjR_Kr6JOE).\
    \ And a blog post by the OEM themselves: [ROS Makes the Difference - A Tormach\
    \ ZA6 Robot Customer Story](https://blog.tormach.com/ros-makes-the-difference-a-tormach-za6-robot-customer-story).\n\
    \nTormach had years of experience with their CnC machines, and years of experience\
    \ with OSS control software. Adding ROS was just an extension at that point (but\
    \ it's certainly still an achievement).\n\n### Pilz PRBT\n\nAnother example would\
    \ be the [Pilz PRBT](http://wiki.ros.org/pilz_robots) (and [on the Pilz website](https://www.pilz.com/en-INT/products/robotics/ros-modules)).\
    \ Discontinued now, but in essence also a \"ROS-based robot\" (`ros_control`,\
    \ `ros_canopen`, MoveIt, IKFast, etc). Many of the packages are still available,\
    \ so you could take a look at how they approached things.\n\nOf course, Pilz also\
    \ had decades of experience in motion control when they started this development,\
    \ and some talented developers and engineers (some even became MoveIt maintainers\
    \ for some time).\n\n---\n\nEdit:\n\n> So I am thinking about the possibilities\

    \ that would be the biggest problem solved.\n\nThe software part after that is\
    \ doable in most cases."

Hello everybody,
I somehow have this indefinite feeling, that my understanding of the possibilities regarding ROS is strongly limited. So be merciful with me please.
I recently got my hands on a six axis “KUKA KR5 sixx R850” (aka “Denso VS-6577G-B”) robot arm. Sadly this is only the arm itself without any controlling unit.
So I am thinking about the possibilities to revive this little guy by getting some AC servo drivers for powering the motors and, from a bird’s eye view, use a computer for doing the controlling tasks.
So… is ROS the right approach for such a task? Or is ROS only thought for using complete robots with their fully functional controling units, to integrate them in complex environments and do their interactions with other machines?
