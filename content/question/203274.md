      Also worth noting, it seems that sometimes if you call `lookupTransform` too quickly after `init_node` you will get the error shown in the original question. To deal with this, you can use `waitForTransform` first, e.g.:

          listener = tf.TransformListener()
          listener.waitForTransform("/frame1", "/frame2", rospy.Time(), rospy.Duration(4.0))
          (trans, rot) = listener.lookupTransform("/frame1", "/frame2", rospy.Time(0))

  body_markdown: "As a first debugging step, I'd recommend wrapping your call to `lookupTransform`\
    \ in a try-except block. Just like they do in lines 23-26 of the [Python tf listener\
    \ tutorial](http://wiki.ros.org/tf/Tutorials/Writing%20a%20tf%20listener%20%28Python%29#The_Code).\
    \ After the creation of the listener, it usually takes a few seconds to negotiate\
    \ all of the connections, and to get the listener to start filling its buffers.\
    \ I'm guessing that you are seeing your error because you are asking for a transform\
    \ too soon after the creation of the listener. \n\nAnother thing you could do\
    \ is [wait for the transform](http://wiki.ros.org/tf/Tutorials/tf%20and%20Time%20%28Python%29#tf.2BAC8-Tutorials.2BAC8-tf_and_Time_.28C.2B-.2B-.29-1.Wait_for_transforms)\
    \ to become available before requesting a lookup. \n\nAs a final note, be aware\
    \ that even if all of your tf code is functioning properly, due to the asynchronous\
    \ nature of ROS, your call to `lookupTransform` is likely to fail occasionally.\
    \ This is usually because of timing issues. I always wrap calls to `lookupTransform`\
    \ in a try and except block of some sort. "
