  body_markdown: "You can bind multiple dedicated parameters to a callback.\nThe key\
    \ is [boost::bind](http://www.boost.org/doc/libs/1_60_0/libs/bind/doc/html/bind.html).\
    \ \n\nThe subscriber interface provides an overloaded initialization function\
    \ accepting a boost::function object.\nA boost::function object combines the possibility\
    \ to accept both functions and [functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_object).\n\
    \nboost::bind creates a functor that includes all your extra parameters that should\
    \ be passed to the callback.\n\nYou can initiate your subscriber as follows:\n\
    \n    ros::Subscriber sub = n.subscribe<sensor_msgs::Image> (\"image_topic\",\
    \ 10, \n                                       boost::bind(processImagecallback,\
    \ _1, argc, argv) );\n\n`_1` constitutes a placeholder for the original argument\
    \ that is passed to the callback (the msg-data (image) here).\nIf you want to\
    \ pass a reference to boost::bind use `boost::ref(x)` or `boost::cref(x)` (refer\
    \ to the boost manual).\nboost::bind works also for class methods (please also\
    \ refer to the boost::bind manual).\n\nSince you want to subscribe to an image\
    \ topic, you might take a look at [image_transport](http://wiki.ros.org/image_transport/Tutorials/SubscribingToImages).\
    \ You can directly apply boost:bind according to the above example.\n\nThere are\
    \ also other Questions here regarding callback arguments, e.g. [here](http://answers.ros.org/question/12045/how-to-deliver-arguments-to-a-callback-function/).\n\
    \n\nPS: Starting from C++11 [std::bind](http://de.cppreference.com/w/cpp/utility/functional/bind)\
    \ is available in the standard library. Currently, ROS does not provide the corresponding\
    \ C++11 interface, since it relies on C++03 (see [REP003](http://www.ros.org/reps/rep-0003.html)).\
    \ However, boost works fine here, but you cannot mix it).\n\nPPS: According to\
    \ Thomas D.'s answer, I also recommend to use ROS-Parameters and launch file arguments\
    \ instead of relying on the calling arguments of your binary.\n\n"
