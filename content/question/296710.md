    , I would actually use approach 1, name the topic `/diagnostics` and use a `diagnostic_aggregator`\
    \ to do the separation.\n\nFor exactly this use-case the [diagnostics system](http://wiki.ros.org/diagnostics)\
    \ has been designed:\n\n> The diagnostics system is designed to\n> collect information\
    \ from hardware\n> drivers and robot hardware to users\n> and operators for analysis,\n\
    > troubleshooting, and logging. The\n> diagnostics stack contains tools for\n\
    > collecting, publishing, analyzing and\n> viewing diagnostics data.\n> \n> The\
    \ diagnostics toolchain is built\n> around the /diagnostics topic. On this\n>\
    \ topic, hardware drivers and devices\n> publish\n> diagnostic_msgs/DiagnosticArray\n\
    > messages with the device names, status\n> and specific data points.\n> \n> The\
    \ diagnostic_updater and self_test\n> packages allow nodes to collect and\n> publish\
    \ diagnostics data. The\n> diagnostic_aggregator can categorize\n> and analyze\
    \ diagnostics at runtime.\n> Operators and developers can view the\n> diagnostics\
    \ data using the\n> rqt_robot_monitor package. The\n> diagnostic_analysis package\
    \ can\n> convert diagnostics logs to CSV files\n> for examination and after-the-fact\n\
    > analysis."

 Hi!

I guess I have a quite general question. Imagine the following: You have a list of sensors that all publish some sort of status message. What is the more "rossy" way of publishing and subscribing to this status message?

 1. All the sensors publish to the same topic and a potential subscriber later filters for its messages of interest?
 2. Each sensor publishes to an individual topic and a later subscriber will actually have one subscriber for each sensor of interest?

What do you consider "better" and why? Thanks!
