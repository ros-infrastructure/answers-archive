  body_markdown: "It's a common \"issue\" with gmapping, it's not really an issue\
    \ because that is how the algorithm is working. \nIn your video we can see that\
    \ your robot has a limited angle of view (about `90\xB0`, from `-45\xB0` to `45\xB0\
    ` apparently), so when your robot is following a straight wall at its right it\
    \ doesn't detect anything else at his left (because of how far your walls are\
    \ between each other). \n\nWhat `gmapping` does for localization is comparing\
    \ what the sensor is detecting with the map it is building. In your case the sensor\
    \ always have the similar data because your wall is straight, that wouldn't be\
    \ problematic if the robot encounter another obstacle a little further or if you\
    \ could see the wall behind the robot. But your robot is getting always the same\
    \ data and after a while it will consider that it's an issue, the algorithm will\
    \ consider that the odometry data has been wrong (because odometry data can be\
    \ very noisy, `gmapping` will give priority to the scan data over it) and will\
    \ correct the robot position accordingly.\n\nYou have some options to avoid this\
    \ :\n\n - If you are just using your simulation to test the `mapping` and don't\
    \ care to change your `gazebo` world, you can add more obstacles that would be\
    \ detected by the sensor when following the wall. But you could find the same\
    \ issue if you add your obstacles symetrically and spaced at the same distance.\
    \ Here's a picture to explain it better :\n\n![image description](https://ftp.osuosl.org/pub/ros/download.ros.org/downloads/se_migration/ros/15665496614553262.png)\n\
    \n - You can change your SLAM algorithm to one that give more importance to the\
    \ odometry, if your odometry data is good.\n - Maybe you can tune some of the\
    \ gmapping parameters but I'm not sure about that.\n"

  body_markdown: "finaly. i found it. just add `<param name=\"minimumScore\" value=\"\
    260\"/>` parameter to `slam_gmapping` node. Because my laser range is 5m.  I found\
    \ this at the [gmapping](http://wiki.ros.org/gmapping) information section:\n\n\
    `~minimumScore (float, default: 0.0)\nMinimum score for considering the outcome\
    \ of the scan matching good. Can avoid jumping pose estimates in large open spaces\
    \ when using laser scanners with limited range (e.g. 5m). Scores go up to 600+,\
    \ try 50 for example when experiencing jumping estimate issues.`\n\nThanks to\
