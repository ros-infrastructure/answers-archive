     I would say that's an error in the documentation, since friction coefficient can be greater than 1 depending on the surface (it shouldn't be negative though).

    [ODE takes the minimum of the two surface friction coefficients](https://bitbucket.org/osrf/gazebo/src/14a3e6e94163602588f6965f6123f4f777e2e302/gazebo/physics/ode/ODEPhysics.cc?fileviewer=file-view-default#ODEPhysics.cc-1143) when objects come in contact, so I think the rationale is that the ground plane should have a large friction coefficient so that it isn't a limiting factor, since a lower friction coefficient can be accommodated by specifying a smaller value on the bottom of a robot foot, for example.

    You can also set the gravity vector to have a non-zero lateral component to simulate an inclined plane. That's what we do in this [friction test world](https://bitbucket.org/osrf/gazebo/src/14a3e6e94163602588f6965f6123f4f777e2e302/worlds/friction_demo.world?at=default&fileviewer=file-view-default).

    Did that make sense?

 Checking out friction parameters trying to solve situations involving grasping and a humanoid walking I ended up with a doubt:

If I check mu and mu2 parameters from ground_plane in the different sdf versions they have values like 100 and 50 or 1000 and 500.

Meanwhile sdf documentation (http://sdformat.org/spec?elem=collision&ver=1.4) says it's in range [0..1].

I've tried spawning an inclined box as a ramp and spawning a cube over it and play with it's mu and mu2 values. I found out that anything over 1.0 seems to be treated as just 1.0.

I've tested this on Gazebo 2.2 and Gazebo 4.0.

Here you can see a video of the friction acting like that with a tool I made to spawn a inclined ramp and a cube over it with the parameters I tell it on the fly: https://youtu.be/I_QM5v0xT14
