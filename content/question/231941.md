  body_markdown: "The position part can be copied componentwise without any conversions.\n\
    The orientation part is usually represented as [Unit Quaternion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternion)\n\
    due its numerically robustness, elegant computations and absence of singularities.\
    \ But on the other hand, they are not intuitively interpretable. \n\nTherefore\
    \ you must convert your rotation around the z axis (also called yaw angle in terms\
    \ of the ZYX euler representation) to a Quaternion (represented by a 4D vector\
    \ [w,x,y,z]^T).\n\nThe tf package provides handy methods for converting points\
    \ and angles to geometry_msgs::Pose / tf::Pose and vice versa.\n\n`pose.orientation\
    \ = tf::createQuaternionMsgFromYaw(angle);`\n\nJust check the Code API for more\
    \ methods.\n\nThere are other questions regarding the conversion, e.g. http://answers.ros.org/question/41233/how-to-understand-robot-orientation-from-quaternion-yaw-angle/"

I have a point in x,y, and an angle of 30 degrees, that represents a position and an orientation of a robot. The angle is about the z axis. An angle of 0 means that the robot is aligned with the x axis, and an angle of 90 degrees means that the robot is aligned with the y axis. How do represent this as a geometry_msgs::PoseStamped.position and geometry_msgs::PoseStamped.orientation?
