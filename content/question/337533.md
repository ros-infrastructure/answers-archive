- body_markdown: "Hi,\n\nYou can use a Kinect sensor to obtain a DepthImage from the\
    \ surroundings, adding the additional camera frames to the robot model. Once you\
    \ have built the proper tf tree with the roboy model + sensors you will have to\
    \ use [Kinect driver on ROS](http://wiki.ros.org/libfreenect) to obtain the data\
    \ through a ROS topic and use it with [this conversion node](http://wiki.ros.org/depthimage_to_laserscan)\
    \ to obtain a laserScan available to use in the [ROS Navigation stack](http://wiki.ros.org/navigation).\
    \ \n\nWith the laserScan you can navigate with a generated map with [AMCL algorithms](http://wiki.ros.org/amcl)\
    \ and [Map server services](http://wiki.ros.org/map_server).\n\nOr generate a\
    \ map with SLAM algorithms and [Gmapping](http://wiki.ros.org/gmapping). Another\
    \ alternative is to use packages such us [ORB-SLAM](http://wiki.ros.org/orb_slam2_ros)\
    \ than can perform SLAM with an image input."

    It seems much broader question then only setting up kinect. One thing you can do is tilt the Lidar but that is question of implementation if you just want to move forward. I don't exactly know what sensors you have, but here are steps.

     1. First and most important step is to get your odometry correct. How? Use of IMU + GPS, encoders, Lidar slam (Gmapping, [ndt](https://gitlab.com/autowarefoundation/autoware.ai/core_perception/tree/master/ndt_cpu)), camera slam ([orb2](http://wiki.ros.org/orb_slam2_ros)).
     2. Then comes obstacle detection and avoidance. Both can be done using [move_base](http://wiki.ros.org/move_base). Move base takes pointcloud/laserscan to generate obstacles and plan your path. Good practice would be to filter your pointcloud for noise and maybe ground removal for better performance. Then you can choose and tweak planners (both global and local) for you usecase.

    Hope this gives you a better picture of steps to follow or things to keep in mind.
