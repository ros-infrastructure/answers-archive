  body_markdown: "Yes, both of you are right. The carrot is two meters to the left\
    \ of `turtle1`. This might not be what you expect, but the tutorial just states\
    \ that \n\n> \"The `carrot1` frame is 2 meters offset\n> from the `turtle1` frame.\"\
    \n\nI have edited the Wiki page to make that more clear. If you look at the code,\
    \ this is exactly what we stated:\n\n    transform.setOrigin( tf::Vector3(0.0,\
    \ 2.0, 0.0) );\n\nThe three values are (x, y, z), and by convention, x is forward,\
    \ y is left and z is up (not used for a 2D turtle). If you want the carrot to\
    \ be two meters in front of the first turtle, change that line to:\n\n    transform.setOrigin(\
    \ tf::Vector3(2.0, 0.0, 0.0) );\n\nTo answer your original question: [@davo](http://answers.ros.org/users/456/davo/)\
    \ is right, for visualization, the best option is to do the following while the\
    \ turtlesim is running:\n\n 1. start RViz: `rosrun rviz rviz`\n 2. click \"Add\
    \ -> TF\"\n 3. make sure that the checkboxes \"show Names\" and \"Frames -> all\
    \ Enabled\" are ticked\n 4. in \"Global Options -> fixed frame\" select world\n"
