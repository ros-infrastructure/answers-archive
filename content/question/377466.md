    You will need a controller and a gazebo plugin.
    The controller can be either within the plugin or separate in a ros2 node, which is what you should prefer in case you ever want to build the robot in reality.

    You can find examples on gazebo plugins in this repo: https://github.com/ros-simulation/gazebo_ros_pkgs/tree/foxy/gazebo_plugins

    The input to your controller will probably be an IMU that can give you the pitch angle of the middle part of the robot.
    The controller will need to be cascaded.

    - You can control the speed of the robot by setting its acceleration.
    - You can control the acceleration of the robot by setting the pitch angle of the robot.
    - You can control the pitch angle of the robot my using force in the opposite direction on the wheels.

      Adding onto this post, I found it helpful to look through these links:

       - A ROS1 differential drive node created by Jon Stephan, there's a nice picture: [differential_drive](https://wiki.ros.org/differential_drive)

       - The Nav2 system for ROS2, again, very nice picture: [Nav2](https://navigation.ros.org/)

       - ROS2 Foxy tutorials: [Tutorials Index](https://docs.ros.org/en/foxy/Tutorials.html)

      I can't speak about Gazebo, but for your robot you will be looking to support a few bare-minimum messages:

       - Publish [Odometry messages](https://docs.ros.org/en/api/nav_msgs/html/msg/Odometry.html)

       - Subscribe to Twist messages on the /cmd_vel topic: [Twist message](https://docs.ros.org/en/melodic/api/geometry_msgs/html/msg/Twist.html) and [Previous ROS Answers post](https://answers.ros.org/question/29706/twist-message-example-and-cmd_vel/)


      Most of the ROS 1 and 2 stuff expects to receive TF2 & Odometry messages describing the robot's position, and to send your robot Twist via /cmd_vel
