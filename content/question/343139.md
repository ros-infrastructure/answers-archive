  body_markdown: "1) From this [paper](https://introlab.3it.usherbrooke.ca/mediawiki-introlab/images/7/7a/Labbe18JFR_preprint.pdf)\
    \ (section 3.5):\n\n> When a loop closure or a proximity\n> detection are detected\
    \ or some nodes\n> are retrieved or transferred because\n> of memory management,\
    \ a graph\n> optimization approach is applied to\n> minimize errors in the map.\
    \  RTAB-Map\n> integrates three graph optimization\n> approaches:  TORO [Grisetti\
    \ et al.,\n> 2010], g2o [Kummerle et al.,2011] and\n> GTSAM [Dellaert, 2012].\
    \  g2o and GTSAM\n> converge faster than TORO, but are\n> less robust to multi-session\
    \ mapping\n> when multiple independent graphes have\n> to be merged together.\
    \  TORO is also\n> less sensitive to poorly estimated\n> odometry covariance.\
    \  However, for\n> single map, based on empirical data,\n> g2o and GTSAM optimization\
    \ quality is\n> better than TORO, particularly for\n> 6DoF maps.  GTSAM is slightly\
    \ more\n> robust to multi-session than g2o, and\n> thus is the strategy now used\
    \ by\n> default in RTAB-Map contrarily to our\n> previous works using TORO.\n\n\
    2) g2o may be already used if you installed rtabmap-ros from binaries (apt-get).\
    \ To verify if g2o and GTSAM are supported in your current build, do:\n\n    $\
    \ rtabmap --version \n\n"
