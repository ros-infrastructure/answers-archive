  body_markdown: "When STL files are based on CAD that was defined about some arbitrary\
    \ point (e.g. instead of using the origin as the center of the part, it is offset\
    \ by some vector), it makes it a bit of a pain to include in your robot model.\
    \ If you have access to the original CAD that the STL was exported from, then\
    \ its easy enough to figure out what the center is and account for it. \n\nWhen\
    \ you don't have access to the CAD it can be trickier. For most cases (e.g. set\
    \ the origin to the centroid of the model), my preferred method is using [MeshLab](https://www.meshlab.net/#download),\
    \ a powerful, free and open source mesh processing application (decent tutorial\
    \ [here](https://revthat.com/updating-origin-meshes-meshlab/)). \n\nIf it is a\
    \ one-off part, it might be just as fast to simply figure out the desired origin\
    \ using trial and error, until the part is \"close enough\" to where you want\
    \ it. "
