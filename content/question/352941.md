  body_markdown: "Please see #q303358 for a description of what the JSP is typically\
    \ used for (ignore the `joint_state_controller` side of the question and answer).\n\
    \nAs to your question specifically: if you only have your robot in simulation,\
    \ and Gazebo is aware of all joints *and* you have configured Gazebo correctly\
    \ to publish `JointState` messages for those joints, then you do **not** need\
    \ the JSP.\n\n---\n\nEdit:\n\n> Ok, so I am trying to do my due diligence here,\
    \ and look up your criteria individually. But I am not making the connection.\n\
    > \n> How would I make sure Gazebo is \"aware of all joint\"\n\nDoes a `rostopic\
    \ echo -n1 /joint_states` contain information for *all* the joints in your `.urdf`/`.xacro`?\n\
    \n> How would I make sure Gazebo is correctly configured to publish JointState\
    \ messages?\n\nWell, from the output of `rostopic info joint_states` which you\
    \ include in your question, it would appear it is already doing this.\n\nThe general\
    \ idea here is that the `joint_states` topic carries information about what the\
    \ current state is of the joints which are part of your robot. That's all really.\
    \ Whether that includes just a single arm, multiple or has grippers attached or\
    \ other things doesn't matter. It carries that information.\n\n*Where* that information\
    \ comes from (ie: who or what is the originator) doens't matter. As long as those\
    \ messages are there and they actually describe the current state.\n\nSo the JSP\
    \ could be used to publish \"fake\" `JointState` messages for all the joints in\
    \ your `.urdf`/`.xacro` (fake in the sense that they do not come from real hardware\
    \ or a simulation).\n\nGazebo can be used (together with the appropriate plugin(s)\
    \ from `gazebo_ros_pkgs`) to publish `JointState` messages based on the state\
    \ of the simulated robot hw.\n\nA robot driver node can be used to publish `JointState`\
    \ messages based on the state of your *real robot hw*.\n\nNote the theme here:\
    \ `JointState` messages are used to convey current system state, no matter where\
    \ that comes from. The consumers of that state won't care where it comes from,\
    \ as long as they can work with it.\n\nBut the important thing is that you should\
    \ not have multiple publishers of `JointState` messages for the same joints, as\
    \ that will lead to one message describing the state of `joint_1` as being `0.0`\
    \ with another message stating it's actually `1.0` (for instance). I just made\
    \ those nrs up, but the problem should be clear: a joint cannot be in two states\
    \ at the same time.\n\nThe issue with your simulation is very likely to *not*\
    \ be caused by either the JSP nor the RSP. They do not command anything, and Gazebo\
    \ would not be taking commands from the topics they publish to."
