  body_markdown: "I understand your confusion. The turtlesim node simulates a simple,\
    \ non-holonomic 2D robot, which means that it can only move forward and rotate,\
    \ but not move up and down (in the Y direction). The geometry_msgs/Twist message\
    \ is used to control the robot's linear and angular velocities. However, since\
    \ turtlesim cannot move in the Y direction, it ignores the linear.y component\
    \ of the Twist message.\n\nTo control the turtlesim based on computed accelerations\
    \ in the X and Y direction, you will need to first compute the desired linear\
    \ velocity and angular velocity from the accelerations. Given that you have already\
    \ calculated the change in the X and Y components of velocity at each time step,\
    \ you can now find the magnitude and direction of the new velocity vector.\n\n\
    Here's a general outline of what you can do:\n\nSubscribe to the turtlesim's pose\
    \ from the /turtle1/pose topic.\nCalculate the X and Y components of the current\
    \ velocity based on the current linear.x and the heading angle (theta) from the\
    \ pose.\nUpdate the X and Y components of the velocity based on the computed accelerations\
    \ and time step (delta_t).\nCompute the magnitude of the new velocity vector and\
    \ set linear.x to that value.\nCompute the direction of the new velocity vector\
    \ (using atan2) and find the difference between the new direction and the current\
    \ heading angle (theta). This will give you the change in angle.\nDivide the change\
    \ in angle by delta_t to get the angular velocity and set angular.z to that value.\n\
