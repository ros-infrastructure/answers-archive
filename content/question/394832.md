  body_markdown: "Hi @turkoahmet\n\nRequesting a step by step solution is beyond what\
    \ is expected on a question/answer forum like ROS Answers. So try to keep the\
    \ scope of your questions more focused in the future and ROS related. Having said\
    \ that, let me give you some tips to get you going in your research:\n\n**Robot\
    \ localization**: the robot must determine its position in the environment\n\n\
    How to determine the position? There are many ways to accomplish this and the\
    \ design will depend on many factors such the available budget, accuracy required,\
    \ type of environment (indoor/outdoor) etc. \nTo learn more about robot localization\
    \ I suggest you read this paper: [Mobile Robot Localization](http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rasc/Download/AMRobots5.pdf)\n\
    \nWith regards, to **Odometry**: A basic method of navigation, used by mobile\
    \ robots using the knowledge of the wheel\u2019s motion to estimate vehicle\u2019\
    s motion. \n\nAgain many ways to accomplish this task, one way is to use encoders.\
    \ Encoders are sensors placed on the motor shaft or wheel to help you measure\
    \ the angular velocity of the motor. Please refer to this guide: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/electrical-engineering-and-computer-science/6-186-mobile-autonomous-systems-laboratory-january-iap-2005/study-materials/odomtutorial.pdf\n\
    \nOnce you understand the terminology, your search for what you are looking will\
    \ be easier. And you will find many tutorials that will explain this in great\
    \ details. I suggest you take a look at this basic tutorial with Arduino: https://automaticaddison.com/how-to-make-an-autonomous-wheeled-robot-using-ros/\n\
    \nHope this helps you get started."
