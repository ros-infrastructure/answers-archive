    This is an area of open research, with several research groups working in slightly different directions. So, I am not sure there is a consensus on what is the best approach.

    http://openslam.org is a nice resource the provides code for some 2D and 3D SLAM systems.

    Also, if you are looking for papers you may want to look at the archives for the RGB-D Workshop at RSS. http://www.cs.washington.edu/ai/Mobile_Robotics/rgbd-workshop-2011/home.html

    The biggest problems I see with large scale mapping is managing the massive amount of data coming from the sensor and limiting the growth of the map over time without discarding critical information. One solution is to simply wait for Moore's law to provide faster processors.

  body_markdown: "Octomap and RTAB mapping are 2 popular 3D mapping techniques.\n\
    These two have their own advantages and disadvantages, so we can choose any of\
    \ this or a combination of the same based on our requirements.\nOctomap generates\
    \ compact maps so it will consume only significantly less memory \nspace and only\
    \ need low computational power. at the same time, the RTAB map is built using\
    \ the RGB depth graph-based slam approach.\nThe main feature of the RTAB map is\
    \ the representation of \nobjects as it is in the map i.e in their real color,\
    \ shape, and other features are \npreserved as much as possible.\nYou can [refer\
    \ to the link](https://rulshi.blogspot.com/2022/11/octomap-vs-rtab-map-ros-3d-mapping.html)\
    \ to understand the [difference between octomap and RTAB map](https://rulshi.blogspot.com/2022/11/octomap-vs-rtab-map-ros-3d-mapping.html)\
