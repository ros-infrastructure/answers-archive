    The recommended way would be adding the LIDAR setup to the URDF model of your robot. You can find the URDF tutorials [here](http://wiki.ros.org/urdf/Tutorials). You then just need to publish the required joint state for the rotating joint of your setup and you should be all set.

    A (perhaps relatively complex) real-world example is the rotating LIDAR macro we use for our Tracker robot. See [tracker_spinning_lidar_mount.urdf.xacro.xml](https://github.com/tu-darmstadt-ros-pkg/hector_tracker_common/blob/master/hector_tracker_description/urdf/tracker_spinning_lidar_mount.urdf.xacro.xml). The macro is instantiated (i.e. used for actually adding the rotating LIDAR to the robot model) [here in tracker_structure_addons.urdf.xacro.xml](https://github.com/tu-darmstadt-ros-pkg/hector_tracker_common/blob/master/hector_tracker_description/urdf/tracker_structure_addons.urdf.xacro.xml#L145). Note this setup is relatively complex because it also provides ability to define calibration of the LIDAR on the spindle as well as full Gazebo simulation capability.
