    You didn't, but should've provided a link to the source of the code you show. I believe it came from the <a href="http://wiki.ros.org/tf/Tutorials/Writing%20a%20tf%20broadcaster%20%28Python%29">Writing a tf broadcaster in Python</a>.

    <i>Translation</i>, in this context, refers to linear movement through space. <i>Transformation</i> refers to the conversion of a position in 3d space (location and orientation) between frames of reference. A <i>transform</i> in ROS represents the position of an object in 3d space at a specific time between two specific frames of reference.

    In the case you cite, a fixed elevation (<i>z</i> location coordinate) is given, which happens to be 0. Also, only the <i>theta</i> value (i.e., <i>yaw</i>, rotation around the Z axis) of the Euler orientation changes -- the <i>pitch</i> and <i>roll</i> (rotation around the Y and X axes, respectively) are fixed at 0.

    Putting that all together, as the turtle moves in its own frame of reference (i.e., <i>turtlename</i>), its <i>(x,y)</i> location and <i>theta</i> orientation changes (in 2d space on the plane with <i>z=0</i>) relative to the <i>world</i> frame of reference. The <code>sendTransform</code> method enters that information into ROS's TF system with an assigned timestamp.
