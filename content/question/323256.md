  body_markdown: "From kuka's website at [youbot inverse kinematic](http://www.youbot-store.com/developers/youbot-ros-inverse-kinematics)\
    \ :\n\n> The input data is a 6D-Pose consisting\n> of the x-y-z coordinates and\
    \ the\n> roll-pitch-yaw orientation of the\n> gripper\n\nSo if you know the coordinates\
    \ of the center of your object you can find the `roll`, `pitch` and `yaw` required\
    \ to face it and just specify it in the input.\n\n\n----------\nEDIT as response\
    \ to your comment :\n\n> do 6-D pose means that if the end effector move to a\
    \ certain coordinate, it has 6 solution? \n\nNo, it means that the pose is not\
    \ only `{ x; y; z}` (`3-D`) but it's  `{ x; y; z; roll; pitch; yaw}` (`6-D`).\n\
    \n> but how do I find the roll pitch and yaw required from the coordinate I put\n\
    \nIf you want to find the orientation required to face the object you need the\
    \ coordinates of your object and your end effector frame coordinates. You will\
    \ need to do some steps :\n\n 1. Using the transformation of arm link 1 to arm\
    \ link 4 that you've calculated, you have to convert the **object** coordinates\
    \ from your global frame to the end_effector frame.\n 2. Once you have the object\
    \ coordinates in end effector frame, you need to convert them from carthesian\
    \ to spherical (you can have the conversion formulas [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_coordinate_system#Cartesian_coordinates)\
    \ to have coordinates as `{ z; theta; phi}`. `theta` being the angle around `z`\
    \ axis and `phi` the angle around `x` axis).\n 3. Assuming that it's the direction\
    \ of the `x` axis of your end effector frame that determine what your end effector\
    \ faces :  You know that if the end effector is facing the object then `theta\
    \ = 0` and `phi=pi/2`. You also know that `phi` and `theta` are the same as respectively\
    \ `roll` and `theta` (you don't need the pitch).\n 4. Just by calculating the\
    \ spherical coordinates you directly find the `roll` and `yaw` that needs to be\
    \ applied to properly set the end effector, so just by substracting those `roll`\
    \ and `yaw` to the `roll` and `yaw` calculated at `step 1`, you get the `roll`,\
    \ `pitch` and `yaw` required in the input data of your inverse kinematic code."
