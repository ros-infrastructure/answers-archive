    I do this quite often when writing new nodes in C++.  Usually what I do is place the following little snippet right after my call to `ros::init`

        log4cxx::LoggerPtr my_logger =
                   log4cxx::Logger::getLogger(ROSCONSOLE_DEFAULT_NAME);
        my_logger->setLevel(
                   ros::console::g_level_lookup[ros::console::levels::Debug]);

    This will set the node's log level to Debug.  Then I comment it out after the node is sufficiently developed.  I have never really looked into doing this in `rospy` or `roslisp`, but would also be interested in figuring out how.

    ***EDIT***

    I just realized that this is quite easy to do with `rospy` as well.  As documented [here](http://www.ros.org/wiki/rospy/Overview/Initialization%20and%20Shutdown#Initializing_your_ROS_Node), you can simply pass the `log_level` argument to a call to `rospy.init_node()` e.g.

        rospy.init_node('my_node', log_level=rospy.DEBUG)

  body_markdown: "For anybody stumbling upon this page, if you just want to set verbosity\
    \ of packages as soon as they are launched by launchfiles, the easiest way is:\n\
    \n1. to `export ROSCONSOLE_CONFIG_FILE=\"/path/to/my/config/rosconsole.config\"\
    `\n2. edit, for example with `vi /path/to/my/config/rosconsole.config` adding\
    \ these lines:\n\n> \n\n    log4j.logger.ros=INFO\n\n    log4j.logger.ros.roscpp.superdebug=WARN\n\
    \n    log4j.logger.ros.my_package_name=DEBUG\n3. launch your launchfile in the\
    \ same shell.\n\n\nThe package name must be the one who corresponds to `<node\
    \ pkg=\"my_package_name\" ...>` line in the launchfile."
