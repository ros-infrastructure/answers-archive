There are a few Gazebo simulations that I've seen with a robot grasping an object, and manipulating objects was part of the (DARPA) Virtual Robotics Challenge, so I know it is feasible and therefore can't give up on getting my simulation working.  (In case it matters, I'm using Kinetic and Gazebo 9.)  If you've performed grasping with a robot in ROS and Gazebo, will you let me know what settings, tweaks, modifications, or caveats one should be aware of in this realm?

**edit:**
To provide more focus, let me say that this question is mainly concerned with the contact physics, but I'm looking for any and all tips since I've seen that [even the ROS joint controller can mess up the physics](https://github.com/ros-simulation/gazebo_ros_pkgs/issues/612).  From what I've read online, it appears that modeling contact for grasping in Gazebo is problematic.  (For example, [1](https://answers.ros.org/question/201625/turtlebot-arm-on-gazebo-lets-fall-all-grasped-objects/?comment=202774#comment-202774) and [2](http://answers.gazebosim.org/question/17100/does-the-gripper-tag-still-work-in-gazebo-7/).)  To ameliorate this problem, I have tried changing kp, kd, max_vel, min_dist, mu1, mu2, fdir1, max_step_size, iters, and even the physics engine.
