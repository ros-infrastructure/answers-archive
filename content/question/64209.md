  body_markdown: "If you want to do 3D mapping with Octomap you should get a 3D sensor\
    \ or a tilting unit for your 2D laser scanner. Also you have to perform some localization\
    \ for inserting the scans into the octomap.\n\nOctomap is not designed as a solution\
    \ to the SLAM-problem. If you want to do 2D SLAM with your laserscanner i would\
    \ recommend [gmapping](http://www.ros.org/wiki/gmapping) or [hector mapping](http://www.ros.org/wiki/hector_mapping)\
    \ as a starting point.\n\nBtw: Your min- and max-angle seem somewhat small for\
    \ a scanner that should have 270\xB0 FoV. (And the max-range seems a little high,\
    \ but i dont know the specifics of this particular model)\n\n---\nEdit:\n\n1.)\
    \ Have a look at the [laser_filter](http://www.ros.org/wiki/laser_filters) (and\
    \ [laser_geometry](http://www.ros.org/wiki/laser_geometry)) packages. They provide\
    \ functionality to convert laser scans to point clouds. (Look at [this answer](http://answers.ros.org/question/11232/how-to-turn-laser-scan-to-point-cloud-map/))\n\
    \n2.) Odometry data is added to the scans/clouds through transforms. You define\
    \ a frame for your scans/clouds and have to provide a valid transform from your\
    \ fixed frame to the sensor frame. (Recommended reading: [tf tutorials](http://www.ros.org/wiki/tf#Tutorials))\n\
    \n3.) You have to publish your clouds either directly under the input topic of\
    \ octomap or you have to start the octomap_server with topic remappings (renaming)\
    \ through command line or launch files.\n\nP.S.: Mapping only through odometry\
    \ and dead reckoning is not very robust. You should consider more advanced methods\
    \ for localization. :)"
