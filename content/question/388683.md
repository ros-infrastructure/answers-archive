    \ 17 2010 14:25:00.01 (1276809900.01)\n\nnote the number in brackets: that's the\
    \ Unix Epoch stamp. The 'human readable' version is printed before it.\n\n> In\
    \ addition, I got another column in my rosbag called field.header.sec that intuitively\
    \ should return the seconds.\n\n`field.header.seq` is not a timestamp. Is the\
    \ *sequence ID* field. That's just a message counter.\n\nRefer to [std_msgs/Header](http://docs.ros.org/en/noetic/api/std_msgs/html/msg/Header.html):\n\
    \n>     # sequence ID: consecutively increasing ID \n    uint32 seq\n\n---\n\n\
    Edit:\n\n> but for the data I have, that is\n\n> unix_epoch_timestamp_t0 Out[29]:\
    \ 1632775218853592670\n\n> and unix_epoch_timestamp_tf Out[30]: 1632775297356186066\n\
    \n`1632775218853592670` is not a valid Unix Epoch stamp. Or at least, not for\
    \ the functions you are using, as they will expect a resolution of *seconds*.\
    \ Note the warning `epochconverter.com` gives you:\n\n> Assuming that this timestamp\
    \ is in nanoseconds\n\nYou're likely concatenating the seconds and the nanoseconds\
    \ from the `std_msgs/Header` colums in your `.csv` as strings, and then trying\
    \ to interpret that as a single number. That doesn't work.\n\nYou can probably\
    \ pass `time.localtime(..)` a `float`, in the form of `seconds.nanoseconds`:\n\
    \n    >>> time.strftime(\"%a, %d %b %Y %H:%M:%S +0000\", time.localtime(1632775297.356186066))\n\
    \    'Mon, 27 Sep 2021 22:41:37 +0000'\n\nNote that your format string doesn't\
