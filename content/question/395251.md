Hi, 
I read every post or piece of documentation regarding the whole imu_link saga but I am afraid I am still confused by the behaviour I see. I'll try to be short and precise: 

**Scenario**: I am collecting GNSS and IMU data with a smartphone during a test drive (i.e. with a car), downsampling the GPS data at 0.1Hz and replaying data with robot_localization (navsat_transform_node and ekf_localization_node).

**Frames**
The Smartphone IMU is using ENU frame definition. However it was mounted with Y axis pointing forward (i.e in the same way as you are holding a smartphone when reading a text).
My understanding from the robot_localization documentation is that, the package expects data in the ENU frame, BUT also assumes that X is the axis pointing forward (in the direction of movement).
To me, it seems there is a yaw correction of -90 degrees that needs to be applied somewhere.

**Behaviour**
Again, from the robot_localization docs it seems this should be interpreted as a mounting "issue" that can be factored in as a transform.
Having said that, changing the base_link_to_imu angles (See below) does not seem to make a difference in the results I see (not even if I change roll and pitch values)

**Questions**

- How is that transform (base_link_to_imu) applied to my IMU data? Is it applied to all accelerometer, gyroscope and orientation data?

- In this specific case, would you advice instead to do the correction on the driver/parser and leave that transform to 0 0 0?
