    If by "status" you mean "ported", then yes (in the sense of: ported and released). If by "status" you mean "stability" or "suited for a particular purpose", then I would say: no (not in a single list at least).

    For an overview of released packages, you have two options I believe:

     1. the ROS Index: [index.ros.org/packages](https://index.ros.org/packages)
     1. the status page: [repo.ros2.org/status_page](http://repo.ros2.org/status_page)

    The former is essentially the replacement of the ROS 1 wiki for ROS 2 -- when it comes to release information at least. It's like the *Package Header*s of all ROS 2 packages (ie: release status, source location, short description, etc).

    The latter is more low-level, in that it only shows which packages have been registered for releases in which ROS 2 versions on which platforms, and the status of their ROS buildfarm builds. For Galactic, `amd64` on Ubuntu Focal, you'd check [status_page/ros_galactic_default.html](http://repo.ros2.org/status_page/ros_galactic_default.html). This is not directly meant to be used by "users" for this purpose, but it's definitely a "list of ROS 2 packages with [some form of] status".

    As to "status": you could perhaps consider the [quality category](https://ros.org/reps/rep-2004.html) an indication of status. At the very least a subjective one. This is linked from the `README` for most packages. For `rclcpp`, see [index.ros.org/p/rclcpp/github-ros2-rclcpp](https://index.ros.org/p/rclcpp/github-ros2-rclcpp/#galactic) (on the ROS Index), then follow the link to [ros2/rclcpp/rclcpp/QUALITY_DECLARATION.md](https://github.com/ros2/rclcpp/blob/468cbab1aa9efb0f18b8d3f5cd410d2a9f21b3c6/rclcpp/QUALITY_DECLARATION.md). This would give you an indication of what the quality is (as subjective and ambiguous that is).

    I wouldn't know of any other lists of packages or lists of "status" of packages.

    (there used to be a thread on ROS Discourse which linked to a Google sheet which tracked porting status of individual packages and repositories. I don't believe that's either maintained nor used any more)
