  body_markdown: "The best solution would be to submit your patches to the package\
    \ maintainer(s) and let them be added to the official sources. Another solution\
    \ would be to fork the package(s) under a different name, and keep developing\
    \ them on your own.\n\nYou can also make a local copy of the package(s) in the\
    \ same directory where you keep your own stuff. If this location comes before\
    \ the `/opt/ros/VERSION/stacks` path in your `ROS_PACKAGE_PATH`, all ROS tools\
    \ will use your edited copy instead of the distributed version. \n\nHowever, this\
    \ is essentially a fork where you keep the old name, creating all sorts of compatibility\
    \ problems. I'd advise against this for anything else than a few added debugging\
    \ outputs or an emergency fix if you can't wait for an upgraded version.\n"

  body_markdown: "The easiest way is to do a local (in `~/ros`, for example) checkout\
    \ of a the source code for a particular package, and then (as noted above) make\
    \ sure it appears before `/opt/ros/...` in your `ROS_PACKAGE_PATH`. \n\nBut this\
    \ is not going to generalize well, particularly if you want to distribute your\
    \ code, because then everybody's going to need your patched version of `move_base`\
    \ (or whatever). \n\nIf you're finding bugs, send them to the maintainers. Otherwise,\
    \ a formal fork is going to be a better choice."
