  body_markdown: "I think [this](http://wiki.ros.org/turtlesim/Tutorials/Go%20to%20Goal)\
    \ is exactly what you are looking for. You just need to subscribe your ```/blah/pose```\
    \ to get the goal pose rather than get it from the user. <br /> <br /> \nAs for\n\
    > stops at a 'D' unit distance from 'blah'\n\nsimply set  ```distance_tolerance```\
    \ to your D unit.\n\nI hope this helps!\n\nP.S. This question is not really related\
    \ to ```turtlebot```. I suggest you change the tag to ```turtlesim```.\n\n---\n\
    \nEdit:\n\nYou should add ```self``` to create your instance attribute so that\
    \  other methods in ```TurtleBot``` class are able to access ```self.posenew```\
    \ too. The topic you have subscribed to is incorrect. What is ```turtle/blah```?\
    \ It should be ```spawn_name/pose```so in your case, i.e. ```blah/pose```. The\
    \  callback \u2013 ```update_posenew``` where you update your ```self.posenew```\
    \ is also wrong. Notice that you have two turtles so you have two different poses.\
    \ Therefore, you need to round the pose of your blah turtle not the turtle1's\
    \ one. So simply make this change to your code.\n\n    self.posenew.x = round(self.posenew.x,\
    \ 4)\n    self.posenew.y = round(self.posenew.y, 4)\n\n\u2206.x and \u2206.y are\
    \ thus the updated ```self.posenew.x``` and ```self.posenew.y``` respectively.\n\
    \ \n\n    goal_pose.x = self.posenew.x\n    goal_pose.y = self.posenew.y\n\nPlease\
    \ also be aware that when you assign ```Pose()``` to your new instance attribute\
    \ ```self.posenew``` inside ```__init__```, the contents of ```self.posenew```\
    \ are initially all set to 0.0 which might affect the calculation of distance/velocity\
    \ in ```move2goal()```. So I advise you to add\n\n    rospy.wait_for_message('blah/pose',\
    \ Pose)\n\nafter definition of ```self.posenew``` to wait for the message to arrive\
    \ on the topic ```blah/pose``` or simply adding ```rospy.sleep(0.5)``` will do\
    \ the trick."
