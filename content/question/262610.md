  body_markdown: "In general you will want to make a xacro for your composite system\
    \ (i.e., robots, sensors, environment, etc.).  In the industrial world this is\
    \ equivalent to your workcell.  The ROS-Industrial guys have a training unit on\
    \ creating modular xacro macros for building workcells [here](https://github.com/ros-industrial/industrial_training/wiki/Workcell-XACRO).\
    \  Good macros will accept parent links and relative offsets for easily building\
    \ configurations.\n\nAs an example, you might end up with something that looks\
    \ like the following for you high-level macro that will create your desired configuration:\n\
    \    \n    <?xml version=\"1.0\" ?>\n    <robot name=\"my_robot\" xmlns:xacro=\"\
    http://ros.org/wiki/xacro\">\n        <xacro:include filename=\"$(find my_robot_support)/urdf/awesome_robot_macro.xacro\"\
    />\n        <xacro:include filename=\"$(find my_sensor_a_support)/urdf/sensor_a_macro.xacro\"\
    />\n        <xacro:include filename=\"$(find my_sensor_b_support)/urdf/sensor_b_macro.xacro\"\
    />\n\n        <xacro:property name=\"pi\" value=\"3.141592\"/>\n\n        <!--\
    \ the robot -->\n        <xacro:awesome_robot />\n        \n        <!-- my sensor\
    \ A (mounted to the table) -->\n        <xacro:sensor_a parent_link=\"$base_link\"\
    \ \n            x_val=\"0.400\" y_val=\"0.500\" z_val=\"0.000\"\n            roll=\"\
    0.000\" pitch=\"0.000\" yaw=\"1.200\"/> \n\n        <!-- my sensor B (mounted\
    \ to the robot's tool flange) -->\n        <xacro:sensor_b parent_link=\"$tool0\"\
    \ \n            x_val=\"0.000\" y_val=\"0.000\" z_val=\"0.000\"\n            roll=\"\
    ${pi/2}\" pitch=\"0.000\" yaw=\"${-pi}\"/> \n\n        ....\n\n    </robot>"
  comments:

      An alternative pattern would be to create the connecting `link`s and `joint`s *outside* the xacro macros (ie: at the top-level level). That removes the need for those additional parameters and increases visibility of the connecting structures.
