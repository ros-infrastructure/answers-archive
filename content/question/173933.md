  body_markdown: "What is your use case? \n\nThe `image.ptr<float>(i)` gives you a\
    \ pointer to the first element (element at position zero) in the i'th row.\n\n\
    The call `Di[j]` gives you a read/write reference to the element j positions \"\
    behind\" where Di  is pointing.\n\nBasically this is how element access in OpenCV\
    \ could be done with pointers. However, OpenCV2 provides you more convienient\
    \ way to access your elements such as the `cv::Mat::at<T>()` method (slow but\
    \ convenient if you just need to modifiy a couple of pixels) or the `cv::MatIterator<T>`\
    \ (the proper way to iterate all elements of your matrix)...\n\nSee OpenCV Doc:\n\
    \nhttp://docs.opencv.org/modules/core/doc/basic_structures.html?highlight=mat%3A%3Aptr#template%3Ctypename%20_Tp%3E%20_Tp*%20Mat::ptr%28int%20i0%29\n\
    \nhttp://docs.opencv.org/modules/core/doc/basic_structures.html?highlight=mat%3A%3Aat#template%3Ctypename%20T%3E%20T&%20Mat::at%28int%20i%29%20const\n\
