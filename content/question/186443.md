  body_markdown: "If you want to **avoid** the requirement of **root privileges**\
    \ when accessing a USB device, you should **grant appropriate permissions** to\
    \ your user. \n\nFor example, lets say that your USB device is located in: */dev/ttyUSB0*\
    \ (I am assuming that you use Ubuntu)\n\nIf you execute \"*ls -la*\", you should\
    \ get something like: \n\n    myuser@mycomputer:~$ ls -l /dev/ttyUSB0\n    crw-rw----\
    \ 1 root dialout 4, 64 Jul  8 08:49 /dev/ttyUSB0\n\nThis means that the ***root*\
    \ user** and the ***dialout* group** will have **read-write permission** and the\
    \ **rest of the users will not** have access. Then what you should do is to add\
    \ \"myuser\" to the dialout group:\n\n    sudo adduser myuser dialout\n\nThis\
    \ will **give myuser enough privileges** to read and write to the */dev/ttyUSB0\
    \ device* (remember to restart your session after adding the user to the goup\
    \ for the privileges to have effect)\n\nFrom this point on you **will not need\
    \ to sudo** any more to access your USB device.\n\nThis is just an example, your\
    \ case might be different. But I hope it helps."
