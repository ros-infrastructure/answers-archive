  body_markdown: "Camera calibration, as the name suggests, will try to solve for\
    \ the intrinsics of the camera which could be distortions due to the lens, skew,\
    \ etc. [This](https://boofcv.org/index.php?title=Tutorial_Camera_Calibration)\
    \ link has good resources about camera calibration. After the calibration process,\
    \ rectification matrices and intrinsic matrices are generated. You then have to\
    \ apply these matrices to every new image to correct them. A corrected image is\
    \ called rectified image.  \nSo to answer your question, calibration is a \"process\"\
    \ and a rectified image is the \"outcome\"."

  - body_markdown: "Yes, you can calibrate monocular cameras as well as stereo cameras.\
      \ Both will involve a similar process but in the case of stereo cameras, you\
      \ will have an additional step to triangulate the relative location of the stereo\
      \ pair.  \nChoice between monocular and stereo depends on your application.\
      \ If you can get by with a single camera then why use a pair? You can keep processing\
      \ to a minimum. On the other hand, if you feel that depth information (from\
      \ a stereo pair) can improve your results and you can afford the processing\
      \ then you should use stereo cameras."
