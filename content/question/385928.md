  body_markdown: "> Yes, in the gazebo simulation I did use `joint_group_position_controller`\
    \ as it is similar to the `joint_group_vel_controller` from the real robot. \n\
    \nno, not really. They are actually quite different.\n\nIf you're using the default\
    \ `ros_control` controller configuration as supplied by UR (so I can assume `joint_group_position_controller`\
    \ and `joint_group_velocity_controller` mean what I believe they do), then:\n\n\
    \ - the `joint_group_position_controller` forwards whatever *position setpoints*\
    \ you provide it with directly to the UR CB, so you're commanding the robot in\
    \ **position mode** (ie: it moves to those joint space positions)\n - the `joint_group_vel_controller`\
    \ forwards whatever *velocity setpoints* you provide it with directly to the UR\
    \ CB, so you're commanding the robot in **velocity mode** (ie: it rotates joints,\
    \ in joint space, at the velocities you specify)\n\nposition control != velocity\
    \ control.\n\nA (imaginary) setpoint of `0.01` for a velocity controller for a\
    \ revolute joint would mean: start moving in the positive direction at `0.01`\
    \ radians/sec.\n\nThat same setpoint for a position controller would mean: move\
    \ the joint to *position* `0.01` radians.\n\nThose are two very different things.\n\
    \n> Then when I tested the command on the real robot (the robot being in the same\
    \ pose as the one in gazebo) I expected only a small amount of movement since\
    \ the setpoints are the same\n\nthe values might be identical, but their *semantics*\
    \ are completely different (ie: their meaning).\n\nThis may explain why you observed\
    \ two different behaviours."
