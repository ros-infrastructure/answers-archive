    ROS is alive and well.

    OSRF has been the primary maintainer of ROS for [over 18 months](http://osrfoundation.org/blog/ros-at-osrf.html) now, and in that time they're released ROS Hydro and ROS Indigo.

    OSRF is currently working on ROS 2.0, which will feature significant improvements, including a better API, masterless operation with improved discovery, and greatly enhanced QoS capabilities.

    The ROS developer community is meeting for [ROSCon](http://roscon.ros.org/) on September 12th and 13th, and I've heard rumors that it's already starting to fill up.

    The PR2 platform is not obsolete; it is still [supported by Clearpath Robotics](http://www.clearpathrobotics.com/press_release/willow-garage-selects-clearpath-to-serve-and-support-the-pr2-robot/) and by the PR2 research community.

    Clearpath might not be a spinoff, but we were quite possibly the second company (outside of Willow Garage) to publicly adopt and back ROS.

    Our original statement with respect to the "end of Willow Garage" can be found [here](http://www.ros.org/news/2013/02/clearpath-robotics-to-stand-firm-behind-ros.html), and I see nothing that would alter our position since then.

    We're seeing a continued uptake of ROS across individuals, students, researchers, and corporations, and Brian Gerkey (CEO of OSRF) spoke on this a little bit in [his opening remarks](http://vimeo.com/107503400) at ROSCon 2014 (skip to ~7:00).

    As for the PR2 platform, it's still under heavy use, with some research groups using it ~20(!) hours/day during busy periods. We're also in the process of releasing 230(!!) packages with the Hydro update to the entire community, not to mention servicing and upgrading the physical robots as needed.
