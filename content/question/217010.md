    \ transform. Note\n> that this can even be another instance\n> of robot_localization!\
    \ However, that\n> instance should not fuse the global\n> data.\n> \n> But how\
    \ is possible that base_link has\n> two parents?\n\nIt doesn't have two parents\
    \ in the `tf` tree. Read [this](http://wiki.ros.org/robot_localization#Published_Transforms)\
    \ and [this](http://www.ros.org/reps/rep-0105.html) (read the section called \"\
    Frame Authorities\"). Here's how it works:\n\n - Node 1 (can be `ekf_localization_node`\
    \ or something else, e.g. your robot's controller): produces *odom*->*base_link*\
    \ transform, **and** publishes it to `tf`.\n - Node 2 (can be `ekf_localization_node`\
    \ or something else, e.g., `amcl`): produces *map*->*base_link* transform, but\
    \ **does not** publish it. Instead, it uses the inverse of the *odom*->*base_link*\
    \ transform, along with its own *map*->*base_link* transform, to compute the *map*->*odom*\
    \ transform, and then it publishes that.\n\nIn this way, you get *map*->*odom*->*base_link*.\n\
    \nNote, however, that you (a) don't have to use these frame_ids, and (b) you can\
    \ define and publish all sorts of transforms from any of these, so long as you\
    \ avoid re-parenting. For your IMU, you want to use `static_transform_publisher`\
    \ (I'd use the `tf2_ros` version) and publish a transform from base_link->IMU.\
    \ That transform should define how your IMU is mounted on your robot.\n\nFor `ar_sys`,\
    \ it really depends on the coordinate frame in which the pose data is reported.\
    \ As long as it adheres to ROS standards and is reported in a world-fixed frame\
    \ (not attached to the robot), you can either (a) make the your world_frame in\
    \ `ekf_localization_node` the same as the `frame_id` in the `ar_sys` pose message,\
    \ or (b) create a static transform from the `world_frame` in your `ekf_localization_node`\
    \ config to the frame_id in your `ar_sys` message."
