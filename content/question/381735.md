      So in the second case you have 4 objects to know the transform between: camera 1 (c1), camera 2 (c2), fiducial 1 (f1), fiducial 2 (f2). If you setup the fiducuials in a way that one can be seen by each camera, then you measure (hopefully with decent accuracy) the transform from f1->f2. Then, you do fiducial detection on both cameras, finding c1->f1 and c2->f2. From there, you can combine the transforms together by following c1->f1->f2->c2 and that is how you would calculate the transform between the cameras.

      On a side note, it seems similar to how I would assume the aprilgrid to work. If it has multiple markers in a known pattern in a grid, as long as you see one of the markers you should know your relation to every other marker on the grid

- body_markdown: "It is trivial to do so using any standard toolbox for the calibration\
    \ of overlapping cameras. All you need is one more camera not part of the rig,\
    \ which is placed in such a way as to create overlap with both. Then simply chain\
    \ the transform. The method, including what to do if one camera isn't enough is\
    \ described in:\nhttp://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1185614/FULLTEXT02\n\
    \nThis approach is simpler than using a mirror, and more accurate. Further, because\
    \ the extra camera is only needed during the calibration, you can use a high end\
    \ one, and achieve very high accuracy. The paper also includes a simulator to\
    \ show expected accuracy. \n\nAs with all camera calibration, to ensure a good\
    \ result: \n\n 1. Light the scene well, preferably sunlight\n 2. use tripods for\
    \ the pattern and the camera to ensure they are stationary\n 3. cover the entire\
    \ sensor with roughly evenly sampled observations\n 4. Use a minimum number of\
    \ camera parameters\n 5. non-monotonic lens distortion is failed estimation"
