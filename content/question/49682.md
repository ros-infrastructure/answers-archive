    The Kinect does not use reference images of any kind, its more of an active stereo vision approach. There is a known infrared-pattern projected by a laser (this serves as the "right eye") and an camera with an ir-filter as the left eye. [This article](http://mirror2image.wordpress.com/2010/11/30/how-kinect-works-stereo-triangulation/) gives a quick overview. Or with a little marketing in [this document](http://www.primesense.com/en/press-room/resources/file/4-primesense-3d-sensor-data-sheet).

  body_markdown: "This paper provides a good overview of the Kinect measurement model:\n\
    \n[Accuracy and Resolution of Kinect Depth Data for Indoor Mapping Applications](http://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/12/2/1437)\n\
    \nIn the paper, the authors refer to a parameter Z_0, or the distance to the reference\
    \ plane. I assume this is what you're asking about. They calibrate for the depth\
    \ readings, so if you go through the equations, you can find the value of Z_0.\
    \ \n\nNow in the model the authors describe, they only use a single Z_0. However,\
    \ it is entirely possible that the Kinect uses multiple and switches dynamically,\
    \ but that could be hidden from the user.\n"
